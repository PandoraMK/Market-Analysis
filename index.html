Study Objective & Research Scope

To analyze the relationship between gold and silver returns, and macroeconomic proxies such as CPI, FOMC, and NFP. This study determines whether macroeconomic factors exert a direct linear influence over precious metal commodity prices using rigorous statistical and time-series analysis techniques.

The research evaluates how effectively Ordinary Least Squares (OLS) linear regression models capture financial market dynamics, risk hedging behaviors, and currency reactions across 26 years of market history.

Data Sources & Variables Definition

Gold (GC=F) & Silver (SI=F)

Dependent variables transformed into logarithmic returns to capture relative price changes and stabilize volatility.

DXY (US Dollar Index)

Measures US dollar strength, historically exhibiting an inverse relationship with bullion prices.

Proxy for energy prices and global economic activity and inflationary pressures.

S&P 500 (^GSPC)

Represents equity market performance and overall investor risk sentiment.

CPI (Consumer Price Index)

FRED macroeconomic indicator serving as the primary proxy for inflation.

FOMC & NFP

FEDFUNDS interest rate policy signals and PAYEMS non-farm payroll employment strength.

Economic Interpretation

While traditional financial theory positions gold and silver as primary hedges against inflation (CPI) and currency devaluation, our OLS regression demonstrates that realized macroeconomic data possesses weak linear explanatory power.

  • Expectations over Realization: Precious metals react more strongly to forward-looking market sentiment and anticipated monetary policy rather than backward-looking CPI prints.
  • Dominance of DXY: The US Dollar Index (DXY) exhibits a robust negative coefficient (-0.88 for gold, -1.39 for silver), proving currency strength remains the most dominant linear driver.

Model Limitations & Next Steps

Several structural constraints account for the low R² values (~0.18):

  • Frequency Mismatch: Aligning monthly macroeconomic indicators (CPI, NFP) to daily trading returns via forward-fill introduces noise.
  • Linearity Assumption: Financial contagion and crisis hedging often operate through non-linear panic thresholds.
  • Future Outlook: Incorporating lagged structures, ARIMA/GARCH volatility models, or machine learning tree ensembles (XGBoost) to capture non-linear interactions.
